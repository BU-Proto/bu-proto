## Where is the bloom brightest and calmest, which zones can carry large and small type, and what contrast do white and black type get at the worst pixels (WCAG and APCA)?

### Takeaway
Without help, almost no part of either bloom can carry white type at WCAG's worst-pixel test. In state A the white glyph marks (L 0.94) reach every zone above the last ~17–19% of the hero, so white text there bottoms out at 1.06:1. In state B no mark is white any more, but the picture still peaks at L 0.55–0.58 in every zone above the fold band, so white bottoms out at 1.7–3.0:1, and the safe dark band has shrunk to roughly the last 10% of the hero. The copy now on the site (state B, centred low, with a 0.55-alpha pool and text-shadow) measures 2.0–2.4:1 behind the headline and 1.7–2.2:1 behind the lede at the worst pixel, so it fails WCAG's 3:1 large-text minimum and its 4.5:1 body minimum, and APCA agrees for the lede.

### Cited Findings
**Thresholds used**
- SC 1.4.3 asks for 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for large-scale text, defined as "at least 18 point or 14 point bold", about 24 px and 18.5 px in CSS. Ratios are thresholds and are not rounded, so 4.499:1 fails — [WCAG 2.2 Understanding 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)
- For text over images, F83's quick check compares light text against "the lightest area" of the image. If that fails, every letter must have sufficient contrast with the background behind it — [WCAG F83](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F83)
- G18 measures the background as "background pixels immediately next to the letter". For aliased letters it samples "two pixels in from the edge". It allows darkening the background behind letters, and outlines or halos — [WCAG G18](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G18)
- APCA levels: Lc 90 is preferred for body text, Lc 75 is the minimum for body columns (18px/400, 16px/500), Lc 60 is for other content text (24px/400, 21px/500), Lc 45 is for headlines (36px regular or 24px bold), and Lc 30 is the absolute minimum for other text. APCA is described as the candidate method for WCAG 3, not a normative one — [APCA in a Nutshell](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCA_in_a_Nutshell.html)
- Derived budgets: these are the brightest background each text colour can stand, computed from the same formulas (`legibility/common.mjs`). White (#fff) needs background L ≤ 0.300 for 3:1, ≤ 0.183 for 4.5:1 and ≤ 0.100 for 7:1, and APCA Y ≤ 0.434 for Lc 45, ≤ 0.293 for Lc 60, ≤ 0.172 for Lc 75 and ≤ 0.076 for Lc 90. The headline's frost (#f4f4f6, L 0.906) needs ≤ 0.162 for 4.5:1. The lede's current grey (#c2c2c8, L 0.542) needs ≤ 0.082 for 4.5:1, and **can never reach APCA Lc 75, even on pure black** (its ceiling is about Lc 72). Black text needs background L ≥ 0.100 for 3:1, ≥ 0.175 for 4.5:1, and APCA Y ≥ 0.254 for Lc 45 and ≥ 0.397 for Lc 60.

**State A (glyph bloom), measured** (`legibility/zones-desk1440.json`, `zones-desk1920.json`, `zones-phone390.json`)
- Brightest: the top-centre and right (columns 3–6, rows A–C), with median L 0.22–0.38 and 99th percentile 0.88–0.90. Calmest: the left two columns (mean neighbour-luma difference 4.3–6.2 at 1440) and the band at the fold (1.0–1.3). The busiest zones are mid-height, centre-right (D2, D3, D5, D6: 9.7–10.5 at 1440 and 13.5–14.9 at 1920).
- White text at the worst pixel, 1440×900: every zone in rows A–E (0–83% of the hero's height) measures **1.06:1 (APCA Lc 0)**, because the FROST glyph ink (L 0.939) passes through all of them over time. Even the 99th-percentile pixel only reaches 1.1–3.4:1. Row F (83–100%) measures 5.9–8.6:1 (Lc 83–94). The 1920×1080 results agree to within about 0.3.
- Phone 390×844: rows A–F (0–75%) are 1.06:1, row G (75–87.5%) is 2.0–2.2:1 (Lc 44–49) and row H (87.5–100%) is 8.5–11.8:1 (Lc 93–100).
- Brightest pixel in each row band, against depth into the hero (1440, 1920 and phone agree): it falls below L 0.30 (3:1 white) at about 78% of the hero's height, below 0.183 (4.5:1) at about 81%, and below 0.10 (7:1) at about 85%.
- Black text, state A: the bright top-centre and right zones (A3–A5, B3, B5, C5) reach 4.3–5.5:1 at the darkest pixel by WCAG, but only **Lc 30–38 by APCA**, short of the Lc 45 headline level. The left column and lower rows give 1.7–2.5:1.
- `mix-blend-mode: difference` white text (the inverted pixel against itself) falls below 3:1 on 44–98% of pixels in rows A–D (the `diffFail` field).

**State B (blocky bloom, current and live), measured** (`legibility/zones-blocky2-desk1440.json`, `zones-blocky-desk1920.json`, `zones-blocky2-phone390.json`)
- The peak is now L 0.55–0.58 (no white marks; `col *= .8` in `heroBloomShaders.ts`). Brightest: the centre column (B3, C3, D3; median L 0.34–0.37). - Brightest pixel against depth (1440, 1920 and phone agree): L 0.49 at 81% of the hero's height, 0.34 at 85%, 0.18 at 90%, 0.09 at 95% and 0.046 at the fold. So white passes 3:1 below about 86–87%, 4.5:1 below about 90%, and 7:1 below about 95%.
- Black text, state B: worst 1.3–3.8:1 by WCAG and APCA Lc ≤ 26 everywhere. Black type is not viable on the dimmer bloom.

- Headline: frost #f4f4f6, 64 px/400 on desktop, 36 px on phone, 2 lines on desktop and 3 on phone. Worst pixel 2.22:1 at 1440, 2.01:1 at 1920 and 2.44:1 on phone. Its 99th percentile is 2.6–3.0:1 and its median 6.2–6.8:1. APCA worst Lc 41–50 (99th percentile Lc 52–59). 97–99% of the pixels behind it reach 3:1.
- Lede: #c2c2c8, 18.7 px at 1440 and 16 px on phone, 3 lines. Worst pixel 2.24:1 at 1440, 2.14:1 at 1920 and 1.70:1 on phone. APCA worst Lc 25–35 and median Lc 59–62. Only 65–72% of the pixels behind it reach 4.5:1.
- With the pool removed, the headline's worst pixel drops to 1.56–1.65:1 and the lede's to 1.05–1.11:1. The pool therefore helps, but its radial falloff leaves the line ends, where the worst pixels are, on almost undimmed art.

### Inferences
- **Large type** (≥ 24 px, needing 3:1 or about Lc 45 at the worst pixel) can sit without help only in the fall band: state A below about 78% of the hero's height, state B below about 86–87%. In state B the left column comes closest above that (3.0:1 at A1, 3.4–4.3:1 in the still frame), but over time it doesn't hold at 3:1.
- **Small type** (16–19 px, needing 4.5:1 or Lc 75) can sit only below about 81% (state A) or 90% (state B) of the hero's height. That is 90 px or less on a 900 px desktop hero in state B, so a lede needs either a quiet zone made by the shader (next question) or to sit at or below the fold.
- The lede should be frost or white, not #c2c2c8. No background lets that grey reach APCA's body minimum, and it needs a background four times darker than white does to reach WCAG 4.5:1.
- Black type is an option only on state A's brighter bloom, and only for display sizes under WCAG. APCA rates it below headline level everywhere.

### Gaps
- Worst-pixel numbers come from canvas pixels at the canvas's own resolution (DPR capped at 1.5). After the browser scales the canvas to 2× or 3× screens, filtering may lower peaks slightly, so these numbers are conservative.
- The 15-minute sampling covers the travelling bands (period about 10 s) and some of the slower folding. Rare alignments over longer runs were not covered.
- The phone runs used Playwright's mobile emulation (390×844, DPR 3), not a real iPhone or Android GPU. Real Safari's dynamic toolbar changes `svh` and the hero height.

## Can the shader clear or dim a zone behind the type (a uniform box or a soft mask), and what would that cost?

### Takeaway
Yes, and it is cheap. A rounded-box "quiet zone" in the final pass costs about 5 lines of GLSL and 2 uniforms. It drops the block or glyph cells one by one, in the way the fold already thins them, and dims the light per pixel. In tests it lifted the live copy from 2.0–2.4:1 to 4.4–9.7:1 behind the headline and 8.2–8.6:1 behind the lede at the worst pixel (with the pool kept). It added about 0.1–0.15 ms to a final pass of 0.6–2.8 ms, which is inside the noise. The real cost is visual. At full strength it reads as a dark rounded clearing in the art, so the craft decision is how far the art steps back, not whether the GPU can afford it.

### Cited Findings
- The final pass (`GLYPHS` in `src/lib/heroBloomShaders.ts`) is one full-screen triangle over the whole canvas. It already computes a per-cell id (`id = floor(px / uCell)`), a per-cell dropout (`smoothstep(0., .05, keep - h(id + .37))`, "cells drop away one by one and stay crisp") and the light (`light_`, `reach`). The picture pass renders at 960/720/540 px and 30/15/10 Hz depending on the quality tier. The tier steps down when the median frame over 60 frames runs past 24 ms (`src/lib/heroBloom.ts`, `TIERS`, `SLOW_FRAME_MS`).
- In state B the canvas is 140% of the hero's height (`.bloom { height: 140% }` in `src/components/Hero.module.css`). It scrolls with the hero, because both sit in the same section, so a quiet zone stated in the hero's coordinates doesn't need updating per frame on scroll. It needs updating on resize, on font load and on a tier change (`fit()` changes `state.dpr` and `state.cell`).
- State B also bends the blocks by the cursor trail's wind (`uWarp`, applied to `px` before the cell id is computed), while "the light itself stays put". A quiet zone therefore has to test the warped cell centre for dropout, or wind can carry blocks into it, and the unwarped pixel for the light.
- The repo already has a clearing mechanism for type. `src/ascii-field/textClearing.ts` builds a mask from the line boxes of `[data-fog-clear]` elements, blurred and boosted, "so the copy stays legible". In state B the site-wide trail uses it for `h1, h2, …, p, li, … [data-fog-clear]` (`src/components/Layout.tsx`), and the hero's h1 and lede carry `data-fog-clear`.
- **Measured: the quiet-zone patch.** Box = copy box + 28 px, soft edge 90 px, corner radius 40 px. Cells inside drop out by a per-cell hash, and the light is multiplied by 0.5. Applied by string-patching the served modules; `legibility/quiet.mjs` has the GLSL and JS. Behind each line at the worst pixel (`legibility/copytest-desk1440_desk1920_phone390.json`):
  - **with the pool**: headline 6.33:1 at 1440 (Lc 82), 4.39:1 at 1920 (Lc 72) and 9.67:1 on phone (Lc 91); lede 8.2–8.6:1 (Lc 66–67 in its grey; Lc 104–105 if it were white).
  - **without the pool**: headline 4.81, 3.34 and 7.95:1 (Lc 74, 62, 87); lede 6.06–6.68:1 (Lc 61–63 in grey; Lc 99–101 in white).
- **Measured: GPU time** of the final pass, with the quiet zone toggled on and off every 0.7 s in the same page, about 144 frames each (EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2; `legibility/final.log`). 1440×900 at DPR 2 (canvas 2160×1890): median 2.62 ms off, 2.76 ms on. 1920×1080 at DPR 1 (1920×1512): 1.88 ms off, 1.77 ms on. Phone at DPR 3 (585×1773): 0.60 ms off, 0.74 ms on. The interquartile range in each state was about 0.4–0.7 ms, wider than the difference. An earlier run of separate page loads gave 0.82 vs 1.02 ms at 1440@1, 1.99 vs 1.24 ms at 1920@1 and 0.48 vs 0.61 ms on phone: the same picture, noisier.
- Visual check: `legibility/shots/copy-desk1440-quiet.png`, with the text made transparent. At dim 0.5 and full dropout the clearing is a soft dark rounded area, with blocks scattered at its rim where the dropout ramps.

### Inferences
- **Recommended mechanism:** one quiet zone driven by the copy's rect. Measure `copy.getBoundingClientRect()` relative to the canvas, convert to device pixels with `state.dpr`, and update from a ResizeObserver on the copy, `document.fonts.ready` and `fit()`. Upload two uniforms (box, and soft edge / radius / dim), and apply them in two places. (a) Per cell, at the warped cell centre: multiply `mark` by a dropout ramp, so cells leave one by one, as they do toward the fold. (b) Per pixel, unwarped: multiply `light_` by `mix(1., dim, q)`. The measured cost is within noise, and the CPU work is two `uniform*` calls a frame.
- **Better than a box, at the same cost class:** reuse the textClearing idea for the hero. Rasterise the line boxes of h1 and lede (not the whole block) to a small mask at cell resolution. That is about 240×190 cells at 1440, around 45 KB. Blur and boost it, and sample it once per fragment. The art then steps back from each line rather than from a rectangle, so it reads as part of the field's behaviour rather than a hole. Its cost would be one extra texture fetch; this was not measured.
- **The dim should be tuned to the budget, not to "dark":** behind white or frost type the target is a worst pixel at or below L 0.16–0.18 for 4.5:1, or below 0.12 for Lc 75. State B's brightest light is L 0.58, so in sRGB terms a light multiplier of about 0.55–0.6 is enough once cells drop out. The 0.5 tested overshoots slightly: 8.2:1 at the lede.
- **Zero-cost alternative:** move the fall up instead of dimming. State A, with the bell of the light (`reach`) centred at 0.70, had a dark band from about 81% of the hero; state B, centred at 0.82, starts it at about 90%. Lifting the centre back toward 0.70, or passing a smaller hero height to `bloom.resize()`, gives the bottom fifth of the screen to type with no shader change. The cost is that more of the screen is dark. Combining a lower fall with a gentle quiet zone (dim around 0.7) would probably need the least visible intervention.
- **Not recommended:** a stronger CSS pool, or `backdrop-filter` on the copy. Making the pool pass needs its plateau to cover the line ends, which reads as a dark smudge. A backdrop filter over a canvas animating at 60 fps has to be re-blurred every frame by the compositor, the kind of cost `Glass.module.css` limits to the 46 px nav.

### Gaps
- GPU timings come from an M5 laptop under heavy contention. No mobile GPU was measured, and the quiet zone's cost relative to the frame is assumed to stay small there because it adds a few ALU ops to a pass that is already per-pixel.
- The line-box mask variant was not built or measured. Its cost and look are inferred.
- How the quiet zone looks in motion (the dropout ramp shimmering at its rim as the bands pass) was judged from stills only.

## What does visible text do to LCP, CLS and SEO, and how should the type enter without blocking on WebGL?

### Takeaway
Visible copy makes the h1 the page's LCP element. In state B that LCP is gated by the home route's data loader: it lands about 0.3–0.4 s after the content API answers. That gives a lab LCP of 2.7 s locally and 3.1–3.3 s against buproto.com under phone throttling, both over the 2.5 s "good" line. State A's hidden h1 had let the nav rim be the LCP, at about 2.0 s on the phone profile. CLS stays near zero. The fix is structural rather than typographic. Render the hero outside the loader. Prerender its words into the HTML with exactly React's CSS: a matched static shell held LCP at 0.70 s, while one missing only the text-shadow lost it to React's h1 at 2.8 s. Start WebGL after the first paint. The words' first frame must be whole and visible. A fade from 0 or a clipped line-slide hands LCP to a later element.

### Cited Findings
- LCP is good at "2.5 seconds or less" at the 75th percentile. Candidates include "block-level elements containing text"; canvas is not among them, and CSS gradients are excluded. Elements with opacity 0 are excluded. Clipped portions "don't count toward the element's size." A removed element stays the candidate unless a larger element renders. "Changes to an element's size or position" don't generate new candidates — [web.dev LCP](https://web.dev/articles/lcp)
- Element render delay grows when "the LCP element has not yet been added to the DOM", and with long main-thread tasks. Prerendering or SSR is recommended — [web.dev Optimize LCP](https://web.dev/articles/optimize-lcp)
- CLS is good at 0.1 or less. A new element "doesn't count as a layout shift" unless it moves others, and transform animations don't cause shifts — [web.dev CLS](https://web.dev/articles/cls)
- `font-display: swap` has no block period and can shift layout when the font arrives. `optional` gives "assurance that there isn't font-swap related layout shifts." Render-blocking CSS must load before fonts are known to be needed — [web.dev font best practices](https://web.dev/articles/font-best-practices)
- React's `createRoot` "will clear all the existing HTML content inside the React root". For HTML generated at build time, use `hydrateRoot`, which "attaches the event handlers to the existing HTML" — [React createRoot](https://react.dev/reference/react-dom/client/createRoot)
- `KHR_parallel_shader_compile` lets `COMPLETION_STATUS_KHR` be polled "without potentially incurring stalls"; plain `LINK_STATUS` queries block. The page marks it "Limited availability" — [MDN KHR_parallel_shader_compile](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/KHR_parallel_shader_compile)
- Google's spam policy lists "Font size or opacity set to 0" and off-screen CSS as hidden-text abuse. It explicitly exempts text "only accessible to screen readers". The earlier clip-path-hidden h1 (state A) was the accepted pattern, and making it visible removes any ambiguity — [Google Search spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- **Repo structure** (state B):
  - The home route has `loader: fetchHome` (three `/api/content/*` calls) with `HydrateFallback: PageLoading`, and `<Hero />` renders inside `Home`, so on a fresh load the h1 waits for the API (`src/App.tsx`, `src/pages/Home.tsx`).
  - `index.html` loads the Google Fonts CSS (Host Grotesk 400/500, Inter 300–600, JetBrains Mono 400/500) as a render-blocking `<link>`. Its no-JS block has `<h1>BU Proto</h1>` and is hidden by `.js .static-page{display:none}`.
  - `program()` checks `COMPILE_STATUS` and `LINK_STATUS` synchronously, and the first frame calls `gl.readPixels` to wait for the GPU, which the code notes "took seconds on a fresh load" (`src/lib/heroBloom.ts`).
  - The main JS chunk is 625 KB raw, 205 KB gzip and 173 KB brotli; CSS is 23 KB gzip (local build of `79d04e1`).
- **Measured, phone profile** (390×844 DPR 3, 4× CPU throttle, 150 ms RTT / ~1.44 Mbps; production build served locally with gzip; API answering after 80 ms; 3 runs; `legibility/perf.log`):
  - **State B baseline:** FCP 2.05–2.07 s (the nav glass), LCP 2.66–2.72 s = the h1 (71,640 px²). The h1 entered the DOM at 2.53–2.64 s, after the API finished at 2.17–2.20 s. CLS 0. Long tasks 474–495 ms in total, the longest 179–191 ms.
  - **API slowed to 1.2 s:** LCP 3.63 s, about +1.0 s, so LCP tracks API latency one for one.
  - **No WebGL2:** LCP 2.72–2.79 s, with the h1 painting 70–120 ms after insertion, the same gap as with WebGL. On this GPU, shader set-up added no measurable delay. In every WebGL run, however, the bloom's `data-ready` (after compile and `readPixels`) came 30–45 ms before the LCP paint, so it sits on the critical path of the first frame that shows the h1.
  - **Fonts delayed 2.5 s:** LCP unchanged (2.65–2.94 s, now painted in the fallback face, 65,995 px²). CLS 0.002 when Host Grotesk swapped in, about 3.8–4.0 s, attributed to the headline.
  - **Prerendered static hero** (h1 and lede injected into `index.html` for `/` only, without the text-shadow): FCP fell to **0.70–0.72 s**, with the static h1 as the first LCP candidate (37,674 px²). React's `createRoot` then replaced it, and its own h1 (71,640 px²) became a new, later LCP at 2.74–3.10 s. With the Google Fonts CSS also made non-blocking: FCP 0.70–0.88 s, same LCP pattern, CLS 0.0038.
- **Measured, desktop** (1440×900 DPR 2, unthrottled, local): LCP 0.50–0.64 s (the h1, 160,146 px²); with the API at 1.2 s, LCP 1.55–1.66 s.
- **Measured, buproto.com live** (same phone throttling, real network and D1): FCP 2.26–2.32 s; the API answered at 2.77–2.82 s; **LCP 3.14–3.28 s** (the h1); CLS 0.
- **Measured, state A** (hidden h1; same harness, local build of `59d3542` in `legibility/siteA`; `legibility/perf3-hiddenH1_phone_hiddenH1_desk.json`): LCP = FCP = **1.95–2.00 s** on the throttled phone and **0.16–0.18 s** on desktop. The LCP element was the nav glass's rim (`SPAN._rim`, which has a `url()` background image: 6,992 px² on phone, 32,476 px² on desktop), not text. The clipped h1 never qualified.
- **Measured, entrance variants on the static shell** (phone profile, 2 runs each, `legibility/perf2.log`; each variant prerenders the h1 and lede, which React then replaces):
  - **Same CSS as React's, text-shadow included, no animation:** LCP = FCP = **0.70–0.71 s**. The static h1 reported 74,490 px², at least as large as React's later h1 (71,640 px²), so no later entry replaced it.
  - **Static without the text-shadow, no animation:** first candidate 37,674 px² at 0.70–0.72 s, then React's h1 took over at **2.77–2.80 s**.
  - **Fade from opacity 0** (0.8 s, 0.3 s delay): nothing counted until 1.13–1.21 s (33,184 px²), then React's h1 at 2.80–2.88 s.
  - **Slide up inside a clipping box** (`translateY(105%)` to 0): a clipped 29,324 px² candidate at 0.71–0.72 s, then React's h1 at 2.79–2.83 s.
  - **Blur-in from opacity 0.01** (blur 12 px to 0): held the LCP at **0.69 s** in one run and 1.08 s in the other (73,710 px²; the blur's overflow makes the box larger).

### Inferences
- **Adding visible copy changed the LCP element and made it later.** In state A the LCP was the nav rim at about 1.98 s on the phone profile and 0.17 s on desktop. In state B it is the h1, at about 2.66 s and 0.59 s: **+0.7 s and +0.4 s**. The h1 arrives later because its time is set by JS download, plus the API, plus render. The API wait is pure overhead for the hero, because the hero uses only static copy (`HERO_COPY`). The earlier numbers were good partly because nothing meaningful was the LCP.
- **Fix 1: take the hero out of the loader's path.** Give `/` a parent route with no loader that renders `<Hero />` and an `<Outlet />`, with the data loader on a child for the content below the fold. `App.tsx` already notes that Layout, having no loader, "renders at once". Measured gain: LCP should fall back toward FCP plus render, saving about the API time (0.5–0.6 s live, 1 s or more on slow D1 responses).
- **Fix 2: prerender the hero's words, identically.** A static copy in `index.html` paints at about 0.7 s on the throttled phone. `createRoot` then throws it away and paints a new h1, and if Chrome measures the new h1 as larger, LCP moves to it. Leaving out only the 30 px text-shadow was enough to lose 2.1 s (0.70 to 2.77–2.80 s). Copying the shadow kept LCP at 0.70–0.71 s. So the static markup must match React's CSS exactly, including effects that add visual overflow. The more robust route is a build-time prerender of `/` with `hydrateRoot`, which keeps the same nodes. The inline script in `index.html` that already tags `/admin` can tag `/` so the shell shows only there.
- **Fix 3: start WebGL after the first paint, and compile without stalls.** Defer `createHeroBloom` to a rAF plus `setTimeout`, or `requestIdleCallback`. Use `KHR_parallel_shader_compile` to poll completion instead of blocking on `LINK_STATUS`, and drop the warm-up `readPixels` in favour of polling. The CSS bloom already covers the gap.
- **How the type should enter** (measured above):
  - The words' first painted frame has to be full size and visible. Fading from opacity 0 delays the first candidate until the fade starts. A line-mask slide reports only the clipped sliver. Either way a later element wins LCP.
  - What worked was no entrance on the words, or an entrance that starts whole: a blur-in from near-transparent, which also tested well for LCP, or a small `transform` drift of whole lines that are fully painted.
  - Put the drama into the bloom rising behind words that are already there. The shader's own intro (`uIntro`, 1.1 s) and the canvas's 0.7 s fade already do that.
  - Don't gate the entrance on fonts, as `MaskLines` does (up to 900 ms). Animate only `transform`, `opacity` and `filter`, so the entrance can't cause CLS.
- **SEO:** the visible h1 and the title tag (`BU Proto · The home base for building things at BU`) agree. The no-JS block still says `<h1>BU Proto</h1>` with different wording. Aligning it with the real h1 and lede would give non-rendering crawlers and link previews the same message. `index.html` has no Open Graph tags (observed, not measured).
- **Fonts:** CLS from the swap is already tiny (0.002–0.004), because the copy is bottom-anchored and short. Preloading the one display face and weight used (Host Grotesk 400) with a size-adjusted fallback would remove it and would let the static paint use the right metrics. `font-display: optional` is the stricter alternative.

### Gaps
- These are lab numbers on a fast GPU with throttled CPU and network, not field data (CrUX). No real phone was measured. Shader compile on mobile GPUs can be much slower, which this setup can't show.
- State A's LCP was measured only on local builds. The live site already serves state B, so no live state A number exists.
- The "prerender + hydrateRoot" path was not prototyped, because it needs code changes. Its LCP benefit is inferred from the matched static-shell run.
- The blur-in entrance gave 0.69 s in one run and 1.08 s in the other. Two runs can't say whether a near-transparent start counts reliably as a paint in Chromium 153, and no other browser was tested. Firefox and Safari LCP behaviour is unknown here.
- Hoisting the hero out of the loader (fix 1) was not prototyped. Its gain is inferred from the one-for-one link between API time and LCP.

## What must hold under reduced motion, reduced transparency, no WebGL, and on phones with the glass nav above it?

### Takeaway
Each fallback has to pass on its own. The reduced-motion still is static, so its quiet zone can be tuned exactly. The no-WebGL CSS bloom already leaves the copy area dark enough. Reduced transparency and more contrast should turn the pool and quiet zone solid, because the hero ignores those settings today. On phones the copy must clear the 58 px nav plus the safe area and sit inside the small viewport. And because the bloom moves for more than five seconds beside text, WCAG 2.2.2 expects a visible pause control, not only respect for the OS setting.

### Cited Findings
- SC 2.2.2: moving content that starts automatically, lasts more than five seconds and is presented in parallel with other content needs "a mechanism for the user to pause, stop, or hide it". Sufficient techniques include G4 (pause and restart) and G186 (a control on the page). The Understanding page doesn't mention `prefers-reduced-motion` — [WCAG 2.2 Understanding 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)
- `prefers-reduced-transparency` detects a device setting to reduce translucent effects. It is marked experimental and not Baseline — [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-transparency). Chrome and Edge support it from version 118; Firefox has it behind a flag; Safari is listed as unsupported — [Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/css-prefers-reduced-transparency)
- **Repo:**
  - Reduced motion: `HeroBloom.tsx` draws a single still (`time = 7`, intro skipped), repainted on resize and scroll, with no frame loop. Lenis is off (`smoothScroll.ts`), and in state B the trail's wind is ignored (`const warp = still ? null : trailWarp.current`).
  - No WebGL2: `createHeroBloom` returns null, the canvas unmounts, and the `.hero` CSS radial gradients remain. The same gradients show before the shaders are ready, because the canvas sits at opacity 0 until `data-ready` and then fades in over 0.7 s.
  - The nav's glass already turns solid under `prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce` or `prefers-contrast: more`, and under `forced-colors: active` (`Glass.module.css`). The hero's `.copy::before` pool and text-shadows have no such rules (`Hero.module.css`).
- **Measured: no WebGL2** (state B; `getContext('webgl2')` forced to return null; `legibility/final.log`, `shots/nogl-*.png`): behind the headline, 9.2–11.9:1 at the worst pixel (Lc 90–95) at 1440, 1920 and phone. Behind the grey lede, 11.0–11.4:1 (Lc 70; white would be Lc 108). The CSS bloom's colour sits higher on the screen than the copy, so the fallback and the pre-ready moment are not the risk.
- **Measured: pointer sweeping across the copy** (state B, 1440, six passes through the copy box; `final.log`, `shots/pointer-desk1440.png`): headline worst 2.48:1 and lede worst 2.24:1, against 2.22:1 and 2.24:1 with no pointer. The trail clears the text's line boxes, and the wind it pushes into the blocks didn't make the worst case worse.
- **Measured: forced colours** (Chromium emulation, light scheme; `shots/forced-desk1440.png`): the h1 turns black, its text-shadow and the pool's gradient are dropped, and Chromium draws an opaque backplate behind each text block. The copy reads, but the WebGL bloom keeps rendering in full colour around the plates.
- **Measured: more contrast plus reduced transparency** (emulated together): the hero's computed styles don't change. Frost text, the 0.55 pool and both text-shadows stay as they are, because `Hero.module.css` has no rules for these preferences.
- **Measured: phone geometry** (state B, 390×844): nav top 12 px (+ `env(safe-area-inset-top)`), height 46 px. On ≤ 900 px only the mark (x 28–94) and the glass menu button (x 312–362) show. Copy block at y 464–743, with the h1 3 lines at 36 px and the lede 3 lines at 16 px. The hero is `min-height: 100svh`, and the copy is bottom-anchored with `padding-bottom: clamp(56px, 12vh, 120px)`.
- **Measured: phone contrast:** current headline 2.44:1 and lede 1.70:1 at the worst pixel; with the quiet zone, 9.67:1 and 8.40:1 (`copytest`).
- **The trail layer** (state B) is a fixed canvas at z-index 40, above `main` and so above the hero copy, and below the top bar at z-index 50. It clears the line boxes of h1, p and similar (`Layout.tsx`, `Layout.module.css`).

### Inferences
- **Reduced motion:** the still frame is fixed, so the quiet zone's dim can be computed once for it. In state B the still is slightly kinder than the moving picture at the left (3.4–4.3:1 against 2.2–3.0:1) and the same in the centre. A visible "pause the light" control is still needed for 2.2.2. The `still` path already exists, so pausing can reuse it.
- **Reduced transparency, more contrast:** add `@media (prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce), (prefers-contrast: more)` rules that make the pool an opaque plateau behind the copy, and pass a stronger dim (around 0.25) and full dropout to the quiet zone. Safari ignores the transparency query, so the default must already pass.
- **Forced colours:** Chromium's backplates already make the copy readable. Hiding the canvas under `@media (forced-colors: active)`, as `Glass.module.css` already does for the glass, would remove the full-colour art that fights the user's chosen palette. Then let the `.hero` fall back to `Canvas`.
- **Phones:**
  - Keep the copy's top edge at least about 24 px below the nav's bottom (58 px plus the safe area), and keep the whole copy and button inside `100svh`. Never use `vh` for the hero, because Safari's toolbar changes it.
  - At 36 px a three-line headline is large text, but the 16 px lede must meet the body thresholds. On phones the bloom covers 87.5% of the height (rows A–G), so the lede needs the quiet zone or has to sit under the fold.
  - On phones `.trail` matters less (no hover), but taps still splash it, as the Layout code says.
- **Glass nav over the copy on scroll:** the frost's brightness ceiling is designed to keep white labels at 4.5:1 over pure white (`Glass.module.css` comment). The headline passing under it doesn't threaten the nav labels, and the nav sits outside the copy's box at rest.

### Gaps
- Safari support for `prefers-reduced-transparency` in late 2026 could not be confirmed from the sources reached. The Chrome blog's table may be stale.
- Forced colours were emulated only with Chromium's default light scheme. Dark-scheme forced colours, Windows High Contrast on real hardware, and Firefox were not tested.
- The pointer check used a mouse. Touch splashes on phones were not measured.
- Whether the OS reduced-motion setting alone satisfies 2.2.2 is not settled by the Understanding document. Treating it as insufficient is a cautious reading.

## How should the type relate to the nav, the fold and the transition to black below?

### Takeaway
The bloom's own anatomy should decide where the type sits. The measured dark begins at about 81% of the hero's height in state A and about 90% in state B, and the screen is effectively black from the fold down. The present centred copy (state B) sits at 53–88% of the hero's height on desktop, which is the brightest band of the picture. Type should either sit on the fall, which means raising the fall or lowering the type, or carry its own quiet zone. It should keep clear of the nav, and the step from hero to page should stay a fall into black rather than an edge.

### Cited Findings
- **Measured, state B, 1440×900:**
  - The copy box runs from y 478 to y 792 of a 900 px hero (53–88%). The headline is at 478–609, the lede at 626–713 and the button at 739–792.
  - At 1920×1080 the copy runs from 644 to 960 of 1080 (60–89%). On phone it runs from 464 to 743 of 844 (55–88%).
  - Over the same heights the bloom's brightest pixel is L 0.55–0.57 above 75% of the hero's height and 0.49 at 81% (`zones-blocky2-desk1440.json` profile).
- **Measured, state B:** the brightest pixel is L 0.046 at the fold and ≤ 0.004 from about 109% of the hero's height down. The canvas runs to 140% (`.bloom { height: 140% }`), and the next content starts after `margin-top: 37vh` (`Home.module.css`, "The words wait until the hero's light has gone").
- **Measured, state A:** brightest pixel ≤ 0.003 at the fold, with the canvas running to 125% and the next content at `margin-top: 25vh`.
- The light dims as you scroll, `reach *= (1. - .5 * uScroll)`, by up to half at one hero height of scroll (`heroBloomShaders.ts`, `HeroBloom.tsx`).
- The nav is fixed at top 12 px (+ safe area), 46 px tall, z-index 50, and the hero's art runs behind it (`TopBar.module.css`).

### Inferences
- **Three workable layouts, in order of engineering cost:**
  1. **On the fall** (zero GPU cost): raise the fall so the bottom 20–25% of the screen is dark, and set the headline and lede there, left-aligned or centred. The words then read as standing on the horizon the bloom falls into. This was state A's geometry, with type passing below about 81%.
  2. **In the bloom, with a quiet zone** (about 0.1–0.15 ms a frame): keep the copy where it is and let the art step back from its lines. Measured to pass on all three viewports.
  3. **A hybrid:** raise the fall a little, add a gentle quiet zone, and keep the small pool. Each intervention is then subtle, which suits an owner who will notice a visible "hole".
- **Nav:** keep at least one nav height (about 46–58 px) of clear air between the nav's bottom and the first line. On desktop the centred tab (x 475–964 at 1440) overlaps a centred column's axis, so a left-aligned title avoids lining up under it. On phones the nav is two small objects at the corners, so centring is fine there.
- **Fold:** the copy should end above the fold. The button bottom sits at 88–89% today, which leaves room for a quiet scroll cue, an inference since none exists. The lead paragraph after the hero should stay after the light has gone (the 37vh margin does that), so the page's first body text never stands on the fall.
- **Transition to black:** any quiet zone or pool must fade out before the fall begins, or it will darken the horizon unevenly. Set the soft edge (90 px tested) so the zone ends above roughly 90% of the hero's height in state B. Because the light dims with scroll, the copy only gets safer as the page moves, so contrast needs proving only at scroll 0.

### Gaps
- No user testing of reading order, layout preference or perceived craft was possible here. Layout advice is grounded in the measured light, not in how people read it.
- Real-device `svh` behaviour on iOS Safari with the toolbar, and its effect on the copy's position relative to the fall, was not measured.
