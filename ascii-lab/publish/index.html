The gradient exists only in your eye

147 agents spent a run researching, building and judging ASCII with gradients in Proto's colours. 104 experiments later, one idea held up: every cell holds two pure colours, a flat tile and a glyph of its neighbour. Up close it is type. From across the room it is a soft gradient.

The rules that make it one material

#061a2e#0b2a46#123a5c#7a6ee0#c2407a#f4a2c0#f8928a#f6fbff

Colour

A cell uses only two neighbouring rungs of this ladder. Nothing is blended. Frost is for blow-out highlights. Mint marks one live cell, nothing else.

Glyphs

Each glyph's real ink coverage is measured, and that coverage sets the mix between the two colours. On edges, line glyphs get a small bonus so the drawing holds its shape.

Motion

Slow light, at half real speed. Cells update at 20Hz and the lens moves every frame. A governor steps quality down on slower machines, then falls back to a still.

What still isn't right

  • The hero is less pink than planned. Violet is its largest colour, and violet is the crowded part of this palette. The fix is to push the halo toward raspberry.
  • Sky isn't on the ladder, so the colour-means-size idea is lost here.
  • Phones are unsolved. At 390px the hero cuts off the cap, and the portrait crop needs its own framing.
  • Up close it is dense and busy, and we haven't tested how it feels on repeat visits.
  • Judging was by agents reading contact sheets. No person outside this run has reviewed it, and it was tested on one Mac in headless Chrome with no real GPU or phone.
  • Two-colour shaded cells are old textmode practice. We found no brand system using it in this form, so say "not found", never "never seen".
