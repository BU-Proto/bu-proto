r1/01

r1/01 Mushroom, reflected arc

Gradient map posterised into flat cells; glyphs on the same strict grid take the arc REFLECTION of what is under them (t' = 1 - t): rose under, sky/lagoon over, and the reverse. Where the reflection lands near the same lightness (iris maps to itself) lightness flips instead. Dark ground: glyphs thin to dashes and fade; shadows get harbor ticks, highlights frost strokes.

r1/25

25 Partitive Mushroom

Partitive colour. Every cell holds exactly two inks, both pure palette stops: a flat tile of one and a glyph of its ladder neighbour. The glyph is chosen by measured coverage, so coverage = mix ratio. Nothing is ever interpolated; the gradient exists only when the eye is far enough away. A drifting lens blurs a disc of the field to the distance view, so the two readings sit side by side.

r1/33

r1/33 Mushroom, glyph bloom

Glyph bloom. The ground is flat harbor. Only the densest glyphs emit: each hot cell throws light that disperses with distance like light through glass. Near it keeps the emitter's own hue (rose, peach at the core), mid range it turns iris, far range cerulean. So the halo reads as a spectrum fanning out of the marks, not a gradient painted behind them. Quiet glyphs are unlit ink: faint on dark ground, and where the glow reaches them they flip to harbor silhouettes cut out of the light.

r3/4a

4a Proto Hero

Partitive colour. Every cell holds exactly two inks, both pure palette stops: a flat tile of one and a glyph of its ladder neighbour. The glyph is chosen by measured coverage, so coverage = mix ratio. Nothing is ever interpolated; the gradient exists only when the eye is far enough away. A drifting lens blurs a disc of the field to the distance view, so the two readings sit side by side.
