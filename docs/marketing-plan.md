# Proto marketing plan

Draft, November 23, 2026. Where a person is needed, the owner is "to pick".

## Positioning

Proto is a free social home in Boston for people who build and make things: founders, engineers, CS students, designers, business students, and people with an idea and no team. Any school counts, and so does no school. Proto started at BU and is run by BU students; the co-presidents are Andres Lopez and Jose Cruz. There is no application.

Proto is where people meet, find collaborators and build. It doesn't invest in or coach startups; at BU, Innovate@BU runs the accelerator and competitions ([Innovate@BU](https://www.bu.edu/innovate/)), and Proto sends people there when they want it.

Proto is one community with groups inside it. The CS group comes first and holds what Proto already has for computer science: hackathons and trips, hack nights, wins, the playbook, and the planned travel fund. Business and Engineering groups come later. Investors are where a builder's work can lead once it's real, and Proto brings them in to see that work.

Line for the site and social: "Meet the people in Boston who make things, and make things with them."

### Who it's for

- Anyone in Boston, student or not, who is making something or wants to start, including people with an idea and no team, and people with skills looking for an idea to join.
- Business students who want to build: run a store, test a market, or find the engineer for their product.

### Who it isn't for

People whose only goal is to meet investors for a job or an internship. They're welcome at build night, and once they start something they're in. Every Proto event is set up so that the way to a good seat is to bring something you're making.

## How events are grouped

Everyone events carry no group name: the weekly build night, firesides, and demo night each semester.

Group events belong to one group, and everyone else is invited. Today that means the CS group: CS hack nights, hackathon prep nights, and hackathon trips. Business and Engineering add their own later.

Promotion rules:

- A group event's title names the group and says everyone is welcome, for example "CS hack night, everyone welcome".
- Every event goes on one calendar at proto.boston and in the same weekly email. Group events also go to the group's list.
- Wins, trips and playbook guides post under the CS group's name. Demo night posts carry no group label.
- The join page still promises everyone a "hack night". It should say build night, and "hack night" should appear only on CS events.

## The fireside format

Firesides stay free with approval, as the first two were.

1. Demos at the door, about 30 minutes. Tables for anyone who brought something. The speaker walks the tables before going on stage.
2. The conversation, about 40 minutes, about building: first customers, first hires, mistakes. Questions about getting hired at the speaker's firm are left out.
3. Questions. People who showed something at a table ask first.
4. Food and hanging out.

Filters that keep it builder-first:

- The RSVP form asks one required question: "What are you building, or what do you want to build?" An idea with no team counts, and so does a store or a company plan with no code. Approval goes by the answer. The form never asks for a résumé, GPA or LinkedIn.
- Front rows are held for people with a demo table.
- Members who came to a build night that semester are approved first.
- Speakers are asked not to collect résumés, and they get the guests' project list a few days before.
- Event copy says: "Bring what you're working on. This is not a recruiting event."
- Recaps show the guests' projects. Posed photos with the speaker stay out.
- RSVPs that leave the question blank or say only "networking" go on a waitlist with an invite to build night.

Business students working on something are exactly who the RSVP question is for.

## Partnerships

At BU:

- Innovate@BU is where Proto sends founders who want funding or an accelerator. Ask to list each other's events. Nothing is official between the two yet.
- BU Spark! runs Code & Tell, Demo Day and hackathons, including DS+X (October, BU only) and CivicHacks (spring, open nationwide) ([Spark!](https://www.bu.edu/spark/), [hackathons](https://www.bu.edu/spark/events/bu-spark-hackathons/)). The CS group brings teams.
- Past co-hosts J, BU Cybersecurity Association and Rionium: ask each to co-host again in spring.

Other schools (ask each for one co-hosted event or a swap of event posts per semester):

- Harvard: the Student i-lab is open to degree-seeking students from all 13 Harvard schools ([i-lab](https://innovationlabs.harvard.edu/labs/student-i-lab)).
- MIT: Sandbox gives MIT student teams up to $25K in non-dilutive funding ([Sandbox](https://sandbox.mit.edu/)), and the [Martin Trust Center](https://entrepreneurship.mit.edu/) teaches entrepreneurship to MIT students. The CS group should watch [hackmit.org](https://hackmit.org/) for HackMIT 2027 dates.
- Northeastern: IDEA is its venture accelerator, run by an undergraduate team ([IDEA](https://ideaneu.com/)). HackBeanpot is an annual undergraduate hackathon run by a Boston-area nonprofit, with 2027 dates not yet posted ([HackBeanpot](https://2026-website-main.vercel.app)).
- Babson: the Blank Center runs Rocket Pitch and the B.E.T.A. Challenge ([Babson](https://www.babson.edu/entrepreneurship-center)).
- Tufts: the Derby Entrepreneurship Center runs a speaker series and Innovation Month ([Derby](https://derbyecenter.tufts.edu/programs/)).
- BC: the Shea Center holds an Entrepreneurship Summit each October ([Shea](https://www.bc.edu/bc-web/schools/carroll-school/sites/shea-center/flagship-events.html)).
- Wellesley: guests came to both firesides; find a student contact.

The Boston scene:

- Venture Café Cambridge holds its Thursday Gathering at CIC Cambridge every Thursday and takes speakers and pitches ([Venture Café](https://venturecafecambridge.org/programs/thursday-gathering/)). Go as a group once a month and propose a Proto session.
- Boston New Technology has run events where startups present for more than 15 years and now lists weekly online pitch practice ([Meetup](https://www.meetup.com/boston_new_technology/)). Point members with projects to it.
