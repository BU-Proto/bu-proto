# Part the bloom around four words

**Keep the copy block your teammate added in commit 79d04e1, and rebuild it as four words in the lower left that the light steps aside for.** The title becomes **"Bring half an idea."**, about 100 px on desktop and 80 px on a phone, over one 16-word line, **"BU Proto: a hack night every week, group trips to hackathons, a demo night each semester."**, with the existing **Join Proto** button and **"Free. No application. No experience needed."** beside it. The block moves off the centre, the bloom's brightest band, to the lower left, its calmest; the blocks drop out cell by cell around each line of words; and the fall into black lifts part of the way back, so true black returns along the bottom of the screen. The commit got the structure right: a real, visible h1, one action that names a real step, copy low enough that the bloom leads, and a weekly rhythm almost no peer states. What fails is measurable: **34 words take about 8.6 s to read** against a five-second budget of about 20, the headline could be swapped for ScottyLabs' "The best place to build software @ CMU" without anyone noticing, and at the worst pixel **the headline gets 2.0–2.4:1 and the lede 1.7–2.2:1**, under WCAG's 3:1 for large text and 4.5:1 for body text. That is also the way past both peers: ScottyLabs says one claim three times in 56 words with its art beside the words, Purdue Hackers fuses its pixel title with its art but selects for "highly talented" people, and neither says what happens, how often, or that anyone can come, or lets its art make room for its title. The build is words and CSS first, then a shader change measured at about **0.1–0.15 ms a frame**, then a load fix, because the visible h1 now waits for the content API and paints at **2.7–3.3 s on a throttled phone**.

*Reading notes. "Spotty Labs" in the brief is read as ScottyLabs, Carnegie Mellon's student software group. "Current hero" means commit 79d04e1, the blocky bloom with visible copy, now the tip of main and what buproto.com serves; "old hero" means the glyph bloom with a hidden h1 (commit 59d3542). The peer note's comparison with Proto (only "PROTO" visible) and the ASCII note's cell sizes (9 and 7 px; the current blocks are 6 and 5 px) describe the old hero. Every contrast, phone and prototype measurement below was taken on the current hero unless it says otherwise.*

*Schematic, not to scale: on screen the title is about five times the size of the line under it. Dots mark cells the blocks have left around the words.*

The bloom keeps the top and centre of the screen, where its pink core already sits; its brightness centroid is at about x 56%, y 40% ([art-led note][art]). The words take the lower left: a two-line title, one plain sentence, the Join button with its reassurance, and one small line of proof. Around each line the blocks thin out cell by cell, the move the cursor's trail already makes around text marked `data-fog-clear` ([textClearing.ts][textclearing]), so the clearing reads as the field's own behaviour rather than a shadow. The fall lifts part of the way back toward the old hero's, which went dark from about 81% of the hero's height; the current light runs to about 90% ([legibility note][legibility]), which is why today's first screen shows almost no black ([current capture][shot-current]). Because the commit stopped the brightest blocks turning white ([heroBloomShaders.ts][shaders]), **the type is now the white in the blue, pink and white palette**. On load the words paint whole from the first frame, and the bloom rises behind them already making room.

Read against the research, the commit got the structure right and the words, placement and light wrong.

| | Now, in 79d04e1 | Recommended |
|---|---|---|
| **Title** | "Proto is the home base for building things at BU": 10 words, 64 px on desktop, 36 px over three lines on a phone | **Change** to "Bring half an idea.": 4 words, about 100 px (7vw) on desktop and 80 px in two lines on a phone |
| **Second line** | A 24-word lede in grey #C2C2C8 over three lines; 34 words in all, about 8.6 s to read | **Change** to one 16-word statement in the title's frost white; 20 words in all, about 5 s. The facts stay |
| **Action** | One Join Proto button; the second call to action in `HERO_COPY` is not shown | **Keep**, since it is the only Join on a phone's first screen, and set "Free. No application. No experience needed." beside it in small JetBrains Mono capitals, the nav's lettering |
| **Proof** | None | **Add** one small mono line: the next real hack night, or an honest "being planned" line until one is booked |
| **Position** | Centred, 53–88% of the hero's height, over the brightest band and on the centre line of the nav's glass tab | **Change** to left-aligned in the lower left, keeping today's bottom edge |
| **Behind the words** | A 0.55-opacity radial pool plus text-shadows; worst pixel 2.0–2.4:1 behind the headline, 1.7–2.2:1 behind the lede | **Change**: blocks drop out cell by cell around each line, light dims to about 0.6–0.7, the fall lifts partway; keep the pool as a backstop |
| **The h1** | Real, visible, selectable text | **Keep**. Never draw the only title in the canvas or animate its letters |
| **Load** | The h1 is the page's largest paint (LCP) and waits for the content API: 2.7 s locally, 3.1–3.3 s live on a throttled phone | **Change**: render the hero outside the data loader, prerender its words, start WebGL after the first paint |
| **Motion and access** | A reduced-motion still exists; no pause; the reduced-transparency and more-contrast settings are ignored | **Add** a pause control and rules for both settings |

*Sources: [Hero.tsx][hero-tsx], [Hero.module.css][hero-css], [home.ts][home-ts]; measurements from the [legibility][legibility], [message][message] and [art-led][art] notes.*

All three notes that measured the bloom find it calmest at the left and low down, and brightest at the centre, where the copy now sits ([art-led note][art]; [ASCII note][ascii]; [legibility note][legibility]). Moving is not enough on its own: in the lower left with nothing behind it, **58% of the pixels behind the title still fell under 4.5:1** ([ASCII note][ascii]). Parting the field under each line lifted the title and the line under it to medians of about **10.8:1 and 14.6:1**, and a cruder box-shaped clear zone lifted the worst pixel to 4.4–9.7:1 behind the headline and 8.2–8.6:1 behind the lede ([ASCII note][ascii]; [legibility note][legibility]). At full strength that box reads as a dark hole in the art ([clear-zone capture][shot-quiet]) and the blurred-rectangle prototype as a smudge ([parting prototype][shot-parting]), so the recommendation shapes the clearing around each line, steps it in whole cells, dims the light only to about 0.6–0.7 and lets the lifted fall do part of the work, the mix the legibility note rates least visible. Staying centred carries two more costs. Component marketplaces sell a centred headline over a shader background as a ready-made hero ([21st.dev](https://21st.dev/@componentry/components/hero-geometric); [Framer](https://www.framer.com/marketplace/components/webgl-orb-hero/)), and a 64 px title over a 19 px lede is a 3.4:1 size ratio, against 3.7:1 to 11:1 in the award-level heroes measured ([art-led note][art]).

Against the two named peers, the changed hero takes the ground they leave open. ScottyLabs says one idea three times (its name, "The best place to build software @ CMU" and a 28-word mission paragraph) in 56 first-screen words, leads with a mailing-list button, sets its code-glyph Scottie dog beside the words, loses the dog below the buttons on a phone, and shows its title as a span while its h1s sit below the fold ([ScottyLabs](https://scottylabs.org/); [peer note][peers]). Purdue Hackers is the stronger of the two, because its 136 px pixel h1 belongs to the same system as its art and it has one action, but its one line, "We back highly talented and curious individuals…", selects where Proto invites, a turn from its own 2022 "find your people" ([Purdue Hackers](https://www.purduehackers.com/); [2022 redesign post](https://blog.purduehackers.com/posts/2022-homepage)). Across 37 student first screens, **only 2 state a rhythm, only Hack Club puts a reassurance beside its action, none says free or no application, and none lets a title live inside a lit, moving scene** ([peer note][peers]). Glyphs alone will not set Proto apart, since code glyphs, pixels and terminal looks are common among peers, ScottyLabs' blue-to-red dog included; light that makes room for the words is what none of them has.

Runner-up A is the most ownable composition here: no peer and no award-level hero builds its title from its own glyph field, and on the current hero the ASCII note's prototypes drew a 280 px "Proto" from the field's cells that read clearly on desktop, and a cut-out version that held up on a phone ([drawn][shot-drawn]; [cut-out][shot-knockout]; [ASCII note][ascii]). It loses on the words, not the look. A name as the title spends the largest type on a word that explains nothing, which NN/g accepts only when the name makes the offering obvious, and "BU Proto is a working name" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/); [message note][message]). If it is built, the h1 stays the sentence in real text and the drawn word stays decoration, never scrambled inside the h1 ([ASCII note][ascii]); the note's size floor for drawn letters, about 150 px on desktop and 120 px on a phone, was worked out from the old hero's 9 and 7 px cells and has not been re-measured on the current 6 and 5 px blocks. Runner-up B is the cheap road to the recommendation, not a rival to it. The status quo with a darker pool is not on the list at all: the pool passes only when its darkest part covers the ends of each line, which reads as a smudge ([legibility note][legibility]).

## Twenty words say what Proto is, who it's for and what to do

| Slot | Exact words | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Title (h1) | **Bring half an idea.** | Break it as "Bring half / an idea.", never with "idea." alone on a line |
| Statement | **BU Proto: a hack night every week, group trips to hackathons, a demo night each semester.** | 16 words; a skimming reader catches "BU Proto" and "hack night" first |
| Action | **Join Proto**, to /join | The same label as the nav and the join page |
| Beside the action | **Free. No application. No experience needed.** | Answers beginners' nerves where the decision is made |
| Proof line | **Next hack night · {day, date} · {time} · {place} · Add to calendar**, from real events in the database; until one exists, **Hack nights are being planned now. Join and we'll email you before the first one.** | Never the sample schedule, never other groups' events |
| Page title | **BU Proto · Hack nights, hackathon trips and demo nights at BU** | Replaces "The home base for building things at BU" in Home.tsx; the no-JavaScript `<h1>BU Proto</h1>` in index.html should match the new h1 |
| Alternatives | **Bring half an idea. Build the rest together.** with "BU Proto: a hack night every week, with food and people to ask." if the owner wants "build" in the title; **Nobody arrives ready.** with "Come anyway. BU Proto is a free hack night every week for any BU student." for a warmer voice | Both scored 3 of 3 on the five-second questions in the message note's desk test |
| Control | **Build things at BU.** with the same statement | Clear but generic: Spark! could say it, and it shares ScottyLabs' frame |

Twenty words is the five-second budget at the average adult silent reading speed of 238 words a minute ([Brysbaert 2019](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/linguistics/2019-brysbaert.pdf)); today's 34 take about 8.6 s, so a five-second reader stops halfway through the lede's list ([message note][message]). "Bring half an idea." is the line to own because it answers the barrier students report most: among students who knew about hackathons and had never gone, **fear of being a beginner was the top deterrent, cited by 65% of women and 33% of men**, and students who came without a team or idea struggled to find one ([Warner & Guo 2017](https://jeremywrnr.com/papers/student-perceptions-of-college-hackathons_ICER-2017.pdf)). The phrase is already Proto's own ("You come to a hack night with half an idea" ([home.ts][home-ts])), no other brand uses it, it names no date that could slip, and it passes NN/g's opposite test, since its opposite, "bring a finished plan", is roughly what selective programs say ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tagline-blues-whats-the-site-about/); [message note][message]). Today's headline fails the matching blind test: swap BU for CMU and it is ScottyLabs' line. Keep out size claims, "home base" or "the place to build at BU", "build with us" (Purdue's only button), "club", "BU's" and "official" ([peer note][peers]; [message note][message]).

One caveat sits under the statement. The weekly slot is not booked (the Thursday 7–10 PM time at CDS is placeholder copy), the live calendar holds a single co-hosted panel, and none of the 38 listed hackathons shows anyone going yet ([message note][message]). The statement describes the plan, as today's lede already does, and the proof line says where things stand; if trips are not running at launch, swap the middle clause. Once a hack night is booked, the proof line should give its date, time and place together, the combination that raised follow-through in a vaccination study where a date alone did not ([Milkman et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3127912)). Before shipping, run the message note's five-second test: 5–8 BU students, at least half outside computer science, see each version for five seconds on a phone, "Bring half an idea." against the control "Build things at BU." with the same statement, and the bar is four of five describing a weekly build or hack night at BU and three of five remembering the title ([message note][message]).

## On a phone the parting matters more than the type size

The phone is where today's hero is weakest and where the change pays most. The headline now sits at 36 px over three lines, and the 16 px lede falls to **1.70:1** at the worst pixel ([legibility note][legibility]). "Bring half an idea." fits two lines at up to **89 px** on a 390 px phone ([message note][message]), so set it near 80 px, with the words in roughly the lower half of the screen and the bloom in the upper half. Size does not rescue the statement, though. The bloom lights about 87.5% of the phone hero, and white type passes 4.5:1 only below about 90% of its height ([legibility note][legibility]), so the parting is what makes the line under the title readable: a lower-left sentence measured a median of 3.66:1 without parting and **12.09:1 with it**, with the line under it at 14.61:1 ([ASCII note][ascii]; [phone prototype][shot-parting-phone]). The message note's phone mock-ups show the same thing by eye, the 17 px statement breaking up over the pink core while the big title still reads ([mock-ups][shot-phone-overlays]).

The phone nav shows only the mark and a menu button, so the hero's Join Proto is the only Join on that first screen and has to stay. Keep the first line at least about 24 px below the nav (58 px plus the safe area), keep the whole block inside `100svh`, and let lines wrap with `text-wrap: balance`, since a 40 px line that was not allowed to wrap overflowed the 390 px screen in one prototype ([legibility note][legibility]; [ASCII note][ascii]). The phone then gets a composition of its own, bloom above and words below, where ScottyLabs pushes its art below the fold and BU Spark! and V1 overlap or clip their titles ([peer note][peers]).

## Words and CSS ship first; the shader costs about 0.15 ms

| Step | What changes | Cost | What it buys |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Words and layout** | `HERO_COPY` in home.ts; Hero.tsx shows the reassurance and proof line; Hero.module.css moves the block left, scales the title and whitens the statement; the page `<title>` in Home.tsx and the no-JavaScript h1 in index.html take the new words | Content and CSS only | The message, and a layout that no longer reads as a template; contrast still fails |
| **2. Parting and a lifted fall** | The `GLYPHS` pass in heroBloomShaders.ts reads a mask of the h1 and statement lines, built the way textClearing.ts already builds one; cells drop out where the cursor's wind has carried them, and the light dims at each pixel's own position; the light's falloff (`reach`) moves partway from 0.82 back toward the old 0.70; the mask rebuilds on resize, once fonts load (`document.fonts.ready`) and in `fit()` | The box version measured at about 5 lines of GLSL, 2 uniforms and +0.1–0.15 ms a frame, within noise; the line-shaped mask adds one texture read, not yet measured | The box version measured 4.4–9.7:1 behind the headline and 8.2–8.6:1 behind the lede at the worst pixel |
| **3. Load** | The home page (`/`) gets a parent route with no data loader that renders the hero; its words are prerendered with exactly React's CSS, text-shadow included, or prerendered at build time and hydrated; WebGL starts after the first paint and compiles without blocking (`KHR_parallel_shader_compile`) | Routing and build changes | A static copy that matched React exactly held LCP at 0.70 s, against 2.7 s on the same throttled phone |
| **4. Access** | A visible pause for the light, reusing the reduced-motion still; reduced-transparency and more-contrast rules that make the pool opaque and the dimming stronger; the canvas hidden in forced-colours mode | Small | Meets WCAG 2.2.2; the default still has to pass alone, because Safari may not support the reduced-transparency setting |
| **5. Proof** | The five-second test against the control, and contrast measured again over a full bloom cycle at 1440, 1920 and 390 px wide | 5–8 students | Evidence the title beats "Build things at BU." |

*Sources: [legibility note][legibility] for steps 2–4, [ASCII note][ascii] for the mask, [message note][message] for step 5.*

Steps 1 and 2 belong in one release, because step 1 alone fixes the words but not the contrast. Step 3 repairs a slowdown the visible copy introduced: with the old hidden h1, the largest paint was the nav's rim at about 2.0 s on the throttled-phone test, and the visible h1 now lands about 0.7 s later because the hero renders inside the home page's data loader ([legibility note][legibility]). The same tests set the entrance rule. A fade from invisible or a line sliding up from behind a mask handed the LCP to a later element, so the words appear whole and the motion belongs to the bloom ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/lcp); [legibility note][legibility]). Because the bloom moves for more than five seconds beside text, WCAG 2.2.2 expects a visible way to pause it, and the existing reduced-motion still can serve as the paused state ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)). Three things are still unmeasured: how the line-shaped mask looks and what it costs, any of this on a real phone's GPU or in Safari, and how BU students actually read the title. Each note's gaps section lists the rest.

## Conclusion

The words and the light are one decision. The clearing is drawn from the copy's own lines, so every word cut is art kept, and the costliest thing to put in the bloom is exactly what the commit added: a three-line grey paragraph, small enough to need 4.5:1 and in a grey that cannot reach APCA's body-text level even on pure black ([legibility note][legibility]). A four-word title is large text that needs only 3:1, and one white line beneath it asks far less of the field than three grey ones. The fix also gives Proto its signature. Glyph styling is common, but a title the light makes room for appears in none of the 37 student first screens, and Proto's field already does it for the cursor.

The next gain is in the data, not the pixels. Date, place and one action is the clearest unit the hackathon sites have, and they let it go stale: "applications closed" or "thanks for coming" messages filled 10 of the 37 peer first screens on the day they were captured ([peer note][peers]). A weekly hack night in the database gives Proto the same unit with no off-season, so once a real date exists the proof line is worth more than any further polish. Until then, the honest "being planned" line protects the one promise in the statement that is not yet true.
