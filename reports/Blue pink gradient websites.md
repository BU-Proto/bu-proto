# Keep Proto's hues, fix their jobs

BU Proto should keep harbor, the blue-to-pink arc and mint, and stop looking for new hues: the palette is rarer and better built than it probably feels, and the trouble lies in how the colours are organised. An exact match barely exists among award winners. Of 1,736 Awwwards Sites of the Day, only 58 passed even a loose navy-plus-pink filter, and most of those failed visual review ([Awwwards archive](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/sites_of_the_day/)). The closest live relatives are Cerebrium, Studio375, MSI "I want it all", Keikku, Hatom and SETA, and among peers HackMIT 2024, Hack@Brown 2027 and HackHarvard 2026. Almost all of them keep the gradient in WebGL, video or imagery, and leave flat ground, plain text and one or two accents to the CSS. The most actionable finding came from reading Proto's repo: the site runs two palettes. The ASCII field paints with colours sampled from a reference design, the interface uses the named tokens, and the two share names but not values. The field's lilac is also about half as saturated as the token called iris. Fix that first, then give every colour one job, because blue-to-purple on dark is now widely read as an AI default and only a specific system escapes that reading.

## Fewer than 1 in 25 daily winners even come close

The 58-of-1,736 figure is a thumbnail scan, so it undercounts sites with plain heroes, and FWA and Webby got no equivalent sweep. It is still enough to show that this is a narrow lane. Thirteen confirmed award sites survived review: eight live, three gone or unreachable (TOKYO BEAST, Cedro, Jantana Hennard), two near-misses. None holds a Site of the Month or Year on the Awwwards pages checked.

Share-of-page figures are the researcher's estimates, not measurements.

Among peers there is no shared hackathon palette, and six of 16 events checked sit on a dark blue, indigo or purple ground in a current or archived edition. The strongest match is [HackMIT 2024](https://archive.hackmit.org/2024/): navy #10173a, pink #ff86a9, yellow #fbdb79, stippled magenta-to-violet stars. [Hack@Brown 2027](https://hackatbrown.org/) runs a literal dusk from #2c2678 to #643e73 under yellow type, and [HackHarvard 2026](https://hhuh.io/) sets a dark teal-navy #01151e with thin magenta #ff0aa8 while yellow carries the brand. [Purdue Hackers](https://www.purduehackers.com/), the closest year-round university group, uses the same pink, violet and blue family as small labels on cream. Annual events rebuild their palette every edition, so a stable palette is itself a small advantage for a year-round group.

Harbor is the real point of difference. In HSL its hue is 210, whereas [Madar](https://madarplatform.com/en) sits at 222, Cerebrium's ground at 226, Keikku at 235 and Plugged at 247. Cal Hacks, HackMIT and Hack@Brown all sit further towards violet too; only HackHarvard, near 199, lies on Proto's side. No checked site pairs a ground this green-leaning with pastel rose and iris at this lightness. The scan was limited, but the gap is real enough to protect.

## Leaders subtract colour; the gradient is rarely a token

Gradient sites that hold up treat the gradient as a picture. Studio375 and SETA have no CSS gradient at all. Hatom's sits in a 3D world, and Be The Buzz runs an animated canvas behind white cards. In this set, body text is white, off-white or cream, cards separate by tone step rather than border, and gradient text is confined to a word or a headline. Fully dark pages are less common than expected: [Cerebrium](https://cerebrium.ai/), [Be The Buzz](https://www.bethebuzz.co/), [Keikku](https://keikku.madebyburo.com/) and [Madar](https://madarplatform.com/en) all bring in light sections.

Leading tech brands have gone further. Of 16 first screens rendered, only [Astro](https://astro.build/) (87% chromatic, mostly indigo-violet) and [Discord](https://discord.com/) are dark and dominated by this family, and [Stripe's](https://stripe.com/) blue-to-pink ribbon sits on white. Linear, Vercel and Raycast measured 0.0% chroma in the first viewport, which is one frame and not a verdict on the whole site. Linear's redesign explicitly cut the "chrome (blue in our case)" feeding its colour system ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)), and Raycast kept its red but dropped the gradient ([LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/ux-design/linear-design)). The brands still using a vivid field pair it with something else that does the recognising: Discord has illustrated characters and heavy type, and Railway has a night-sky illustration over a tinted ground.

The reason for caution is reputational. A review of 1,590 Show HN submissions listed "VibeCode Purple", "gradients everywhere", "permanent dark mode" and "large colored glows" among AI design patterns, though it gives no prevalence for any one of them ([Krebs](https://adriankrebs.ch/blog/design-slop/)). One agency calls the blue-to-purple gradient "the single loudest AI tell in 2026" and locates the fault in a gradient nobody chose ([925Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells)). This evidence is commentary, not measurement, and writers disagree on the remedy: Envato sees youth brands moving towards "neon gradients" ([Envato](https://elements.envato.com/learn/web-design-trends)), while Webyansh recommends warm whites and one accent ([Webyansh](https://webyansh.com/post/top-web-design-trends)). The same source found dark pages on 47% of recent Sites of the Day, so dark alone distinguishes nothing. The exposed part of Proto's arc is the middle. Blue through violet is shared with Astro, Discord and the AI default, while the pink end and the greener ground are less crowded. No source tested that, but the reasoning points towards pink as the end Proto should lean on.

## Proto's glyph field is the world peers build every year

Distinctive peers commit to one concrete world, and colour follows from it: [BostonHacks'](https://bostonhacks.org/) subway map, [PennApps'](https://pennapps.com/) CRT carried through boot screen and forms, [HackGT's](https://hack.gt/) seaside market. Generic sites pick a scheme and apply it to a template. Dusk-behind-a-skyline is already becoming a shared trope, so Proto should stay away from landscape readings of its gradient. The dawn shape added for the landing page is worth watching for this.

The ASCII field is that world, and its tinted treatment looks unclaimed. Respected glyph sites are restrained: [Ghostty](https://ghostty.org/) uses grey glyphs with a #3551f3 halo on #0f0f11, [Aino](https://aino.agency/) uses a single `--ascii-color`, and [Kostya Farber](https://www.kostyafarber.com/) uses white on black. [Oxide's](https://oxide.computer/) checked animation is flat pink even though its renderer can [colour every cell](https://play.ertdfgcvb.xyz/). Among about 30 peer sites, none tinted a glyph field with a gradient. That makes it Proto's most ownable asset, with the caveat that there is no precedent to copy for legibility, and such effects may exist outside the sample. Awwwards rewarded Aino for a site-wide text mode, not a decorative hero ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/aino-1)), so the field should behave as a material across the site.

Proto has a second asset, seen on no checked peer, in the repo's own rules: colour means size. Weekly hack night is sky, the monthly trip iris and semester demo night rose; Start, Build and Launch follow the same order (`src/index.css`). Purdue's coloured words label categories, which is a weaker idea. Proto's gradient therefore encodes a scale, which gives it a reason to exist.

## The site runs two palettes under the same names

This finding comes from reading the code, not from the research notes, and it was not checked in a rendered browser. The brief's palette describes the tokens. The field draws from different data: `src/ascii-field/colormap.ts` is a 48 by 30 grid sampled from a reference hero design, and `shapes.ts` paints the other shapes from pigments "picked out of that gradient". Four names therefore point at two colours each: `--sky` is #7cc7f4 but pigment sky is #039ac8, `--iris` is #ab9dff but pigment iris is #8488b5, `--rose` is #f4a2c0 but pigment rose is #da346f, and lagoon is #3fb8d0 in CSS but #00a4d0 in the field. The token comment calling sky, iris and rose "the field's own light" overstates the match. The closer comparison is the deep tier below.

| Name | UI token | Field pigment | Oklab ΔE | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blue | cerulean #1a8fc0 | sky #039ac8 | 0.031 | About 1.5 JND; effectively the same colour |
| Pink | raspberry #c2407a | rose #da346f | 0.048 | About 2.4 JND; close |
| Violet | iris deep #7a6ee0, C 0.167 | iris #8488b5, C 0.067 | 0.106 | About 5 JND; the field's is far greyer |
| Warm | no brand token | peach #f8928a, haze #cec1be | n/a | Only in the field |

The ΔE, chroma (C) and JND figures here were computed for this report ([JND of 0.02 per CSS Color 4](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-color-4/)).

Measured across the hero map, 59% of cells are blue, 21% pink and only 16% violet, and violet cells have a median chroma of 0.063, under half of iris's 0.139. Follow the middle row and the blue-to-pink crossing runs #0481b4, #4091bd, #9e8bb3, #e59b9e, #d2567b: blue, grey-mauve, salmon, rose. So Proto's real hero is closer to cyan through mauve into salmon-rose than to the sky-iris-rose the interface shows beside it, and 31% of cells fall below chroma 0.08. Grey-mauve at the crossing is the classic muddy midpoint: a straight path across 120° of hue loses half its chroma, by the research team's computation. This may be what makes the scheme hard to pin down, because a pastel interface sits on a warmer, greyer, more saturated-at-the-edges picture, but that is a hypothesis. The glyph tint and lift change the final pixels, and a muted centre can read as natural light.

The warmth deserves defending. Peers almost always add yellow or cream to stop navy and pink going cold, and the field's salmon core already does that job without adding a brand colour. It has one side effect. Status coral #ffb4a8, the "something went wrong" colour, sits in that same warm neighbourhood, only 0.067 from rose, which is about 3 JND.

Token work is smaller than the owner may fear. The ladder is sound: light and deep tiers share hue within 4.1°, surfaces step evenly at about +0.06 L, and every light accent clears WCAG AA on all three surfaces. Harbor's chroma of 0.048 is 1.6 times [Radix](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@radix-ui/colors) blue2, which is a brand choice to keep. The real defects are iris (L 0.05 low, C 0.04 high, on the sRGB edge), muted (passes WCAG, reaches only Lc 45 to 51 on [APCA](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCAeasyIntro)), coral, and `--field-sweep`, whose last leg collapses to grey #807ca5 at C 0.062. Mint is defensible: it sits 32° from the arc's true complement, and of the alternatives tested only lime separates better under red-green colour blindness, while doing worse under tritan. Hatom and MSI both support one small green. [Research on harmony](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13414-010-0027-0) favours near hues for the arc, and an accent only needs to differ.

## Ten decisions, in priority order

Items 1 to 3 settle the scheme; the rest are contained edits. Hex values were checked for WCAG on harbor, raised 1 and raised 2. Proportions are starting values to tune by eye, since no study sets a limit ([the nearest evidence](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.17460) is about data visualisation).

| # | Decision | Exact change | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Freeze the palette | Keep harbor #061a2e, the sky-iris-rose order and mint. Add no new brand hue, and no yellow | Rare ground, working scale, ownable combination |
| 2 | Make one palette | Treat tokens as the source of truth. Set field `P.iris` to #857fd7 (same L 0.64, C 0.13). Regenerate the bloom map with violet cells lifted to C ≥ 0.10. Compare before and after in the browser, and keep the old map if it wins | Closes the greatest gap, and removes the grey crossing |
| 3 | Name the warm core | Add `--field-warm` for peach and haze, field-only, never UI. Rename field `P.*` so no name repeats a token with a different value | Keeps warmth, ends the double meanings |
| 4 | Assign one job per colour | Harbor and surfaces: ground. Sky, iris, rose: size only. Mint: live, open or yours only. Amber and coral: state only, with icon and text | Most of the felt noise is colours doing several jobs |
| 5 | Set a page budget | 85 to 90% harbor, surfaces and neutrals; 8 to 12% field and accents, in hero and big moments; 1 to 3% mint; under 1% status. Three saturated hues at most per viewport outside the field | Keikku and Linear-level restraint without losing colour |
| 6 | Keep the gradient in the field | No new gradient fills, gradient borders or gradient paragraph text in CSS. One gradient word per page at most. Hue must shift across space, never per character | Winners separate gradient from UI; per-character hue is noise, by judgement and untested |
| 7 | Repair four tokens | `--iris` to #b6aaff (8.52:1, 7.10, 5.70). Muted to #a7bac9 (8.80, 7.33, 5.89). Coral to #ff7f6e (7.12, 5.94, 4.77; 0.111 from rose, and 0.044 from peach, which is acceptable only because peach never appears in UI). Add fills #1678a1 and #7064d3 for frost text (4.76, 4.53). Keep raspberry | Closes the measured defects. #b6aaff also meets the [Radix step 11 band](https://www.radix-ui.com/colors/docs/palette-composition/understanding-the-scale). APCA for coral was not computed |
| 8 | Fix `--field-sweep` | Reorder to lagoon, cerulean, iris deep, raspberry (hue 214, 233, 285, 357). Beyond about 60° of hue, any new gradient gets a mid stop or `in oklch`; plain `in oklab` does not solve it | Removes the grey leg. Support is universal since [Firefox 127](https://github.com/web-platform-dx/web-features) |
| 9 | Protect legibility | Never put body text on the raw field: frost is 3.12:1 on field sky, 2.13 on peach and 1.68 on haze. Text sits on harbor or a cleared area. Inputs get a border of at least [3:1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html), since raised 1 on harbor is only 1.20; muted works | A dimmed field under text is the working pattern, as [HackMIT 2027](https://hackmit.org/) shows |
| 10 | Clean the names and second layer | Fix aliases that mislead: `--c-navy` resolves to iris, and `--c-paper` to harbor. Choose one text ladder (`--ink-soft`/`--ink-dim` or `--c-body`/`--c-muted`). Use `--paper` for one relief section on long pages. Let real photographs carry the people | Light sections and people are what Cerebrium, Keikku and Discord use for relief and recognition |

Reject four tempting moves. Do not go to pure black, as that throws away the ground's distinctive hue, which only HackHarvard comes near among checked sites. Do not swap mint for lime, which is more acidic and off-brand for a marginal gain. Do not apply 60-30-10 literally: it is an [interior-design heuristic](https://www.apartmenttherapy.com/interior-design-rule-60-30-10-explained-37504313), the search found no peer-reviewed support, and it would flood the page. And do not add a grain overlay by reflex: Chrome already dithered the CSS gradients tested, and overlay noise only masks steps rather than removing them. Test Safari first, since neither Safari nor Firefox was checked. If the WebGL field shows banding in its glows, dither in the shader, where [it actually works](https://pixelmager.github.io/linelight/banding); the current shader has none.

## Conclusion

The question "which colours?" turns out to be largely answered, and the harder question is "which colours mean what, and who owns the source of truth?". An owner comparing a pastel token sheet with a field painted from another design's sampled gradient would naturally feel that something is off however often the tokens are retuned. The first test costs an afternoon: change the field's iris, compare both versions on a real display, and let the result decide whether the middle should be vivid or stay as soft light.

The longer implication concerns provenance. Proto's hero gradient is still, literally, a sample of a reference design with its figure and text inpainted out, and the repo does not say whose design that was. Regenerating it from Proto's own tokens would make the one asset no peer has fully Proto's, and would give the size scale of sky, iris and rose a single home in both field and interface. No outcome data links any of these palettes to applications or attendance, so judge the result by whether a visitor can read the scale and recognise the field, not by whether it matches an award winner.
